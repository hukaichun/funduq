## What it is

A clearing design for agentic commerce: six separable questions
(authorization / execution / performance / attribution / loss / settlement),
seven primitives, one falsifiable soundness property. **The nearest
same-axis neighbour** — closer than AIP — because it separates authorization
from attribution the way this paper does, and then answers with an
adjudicator.

## Quotes we use

> RAILS adjudicates on evidence (§5.5)

**Where** — §2.3. **Why** — the axis is not neutral-versus-not, it is
**neutral adjudicator versus neutral abstainer**. Both call themselves
neutral; only one will rule.

> [the subdelegation chain] leaves open the assignment of fault… because
> there is no human principal to absorb it (§12.1)

**Where** — available for §3. **Why** — this paper denies the premise: every
delegation tree is human-rooted and human-backed at every node, so the
problem dissolves rather than being solved.

## Cautions

- **It does have escalation** (§6.3 human-arbiter loop plus an appeal
  window). Never claim it has none — it is escalation *within adjudication*,
  not escalation of responsibility.
- Its human arbiter is demoted to "one more verifier vote".
- Its CANCELLED is bilateral agreement only — no cancel-as-request.
- Its §12 "Governance questions remain open" is demand-side evidence for the
  positioning, and worth citing as such.
